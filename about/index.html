About HorseFarrier.net

A free hoof care and farriery resource for horse owners, a public service of Bridle & Bit Magazine.

HorseFarrier.net exists to give horse owners clear, practical information about hooves, shoeing and the work farriers do. Good hoof care is one of the biggest factors in a sound horse, and most owners only learn the basics when something goes wrong. This site is meant to get that information in front of you first.

Who publishes this site

HorseFarrier.net is published by Bridle & Bit Magazine, an Arizona equestrian publication serving the western and English horse community since 1978. The site is offered free as a public service to horse owners everywhere.

What you will find here

The site is organized so you can start with the basics and go as deep as your situation requires.

Horseshoe TypesShoe styles and what each one is used for. Corrective & Therapeutic ShoeingHow shoeing is used to address balance and lameness issues. DisciplinesHoof and shoeing considerations for nine disciplines, from barrel racing to dressage. Farrier ToolsThe tools of the trade and what they do. DIY HorseshoeingWhat owners can and cannot safely do themselves. VideosA curated library of hoof care videos from around YouTube. FAQQuick answers to the questions owners ask most. GlossaryPlain-language definitions of farrier and hoof terms.

About the videos

Videos on this site are embedded from YouTube and belong to their creators. We link to them because we think they are useful, which does not mean the creators are affiliated with HorseFarrier.net or Bridle & Bit Magazine, and we do not endorse every technique shown.

Finding a farrier

This site cannot replace a qualified farrier. If you need one, start with our Find a Farrier page, and ask your veterinarian or other horse owners in your area for recommendations.

Important: this is general information

Not veterinary advice. Everything on HorseFarrier.net is general educational information. It is not a substitute for examination and treatment by a licensed veterinarian or the work of a qualified farrier. If your horse is lame, has a hot or swollen hoof, a foul-smelling discharge, or any sudden change in how it moves, call your veterinarian promptly.
